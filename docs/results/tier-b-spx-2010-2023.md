# Tier B on SPX, 2010 to 2023, with the VIX futures curve (OptionsDX)

Follow-up to [`tier-b-spy-2010-2023.md`](tier-b-spy-2010-2023.md). Same replay, now on the product StormGlass actually trades: 1,146 one-lot SPX strangles from OptionsDX end-of-day chains, January 2010 to December 2023. Every exit is priced off a real SPX quote. I also built a daily VIX futures curve from OptionsDX's VIX option chains and tested it as a second regime signal.

Setup: 16-delta short strangle, expiry nearest 45 DTE inside 30 to 60, a new 1-lot trade every 3 sessions, settled at the expiry-day close. Raw output: [`tier-b-spx-2010-2023.json`](tier-b-spx-2010-2023.json).

One SPX contract is about ten SPY contracts, so dollar figures run about 10x the SPY study.

## What it says

- **SPX agrees with SPY on the profit target.** It cuts the 5th-percentile loss almost in half (-$6,906 to -$3,678) and raises the win rate from 78% to 90%. It costs more return on SPX: it keeps 64% of hold-to-expiry, against 72% on SPY.
- **Execution matters more on SPX.** Bid/ask fills cost 4.6 points of return on credit with the profit target (19.8% at mid, 15.2% at bid/ask). On SPY it was 1.6. SPX spreads are wider, and the profit-target exit pays the spread twice. Working orders toward the mid is worth real money.
- **Same tail, same month.** The six worst trades opened between January 30 and February 21, 2020. The worst lost $79,959 on one lot. The worst 5% of trades lost $1.0M against $2.1M in total gains. That's why the 5% worst-case sizing cap exists.
- **Same bad years.** 2017 (-11.3%) and 2020 (-60.1%) lost money on both SPX and SPY. 12 of 14 years were positive.

## The VIX futures curve

`research/spx-replay/vix_curve.py` reads OptionsDX's VIX option chains and backs out the futures price for each expiry with put-call parity: F = K + (C - P) at the strike where call and put are closest. Interpolating to 30 days gives a constant-maturity forward, and slope = (F30 - VIX) / VIX. Output: [`vix-curve-2010-2023.csv`](vix-curve-2010-2023.csv), 3,465 sessions.

Data check: OptionsDX's spot VIX matches Cboe's official close with a median gap of $0.09 over 3,458 matched days. 34 days were off by more than a point, all on fast days (August 2011, August 2015) where the snapshot and the close were taken at different moments. The curve reads backwardated in every panic you'd expect: May 2010 (-21%), February 2018 (-38%), March 2020 (-26%). Median slope is +10%, and 16% of sessions are inverted.

## Does either curve predict which trades win?

50% profit target, bid/ask fills. p-values come from a circular block shift that keeps backwardation labels clustered the way they really are, 2,000 draws.

| Signal (day before entry) | Underlying | Backwardation trades | Return on credit | Rest | Difference | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| VIX3M / VIX (shipping classifier) | SPX | 73 | 23.3% | 14.5% | +8.8 pts | 0.82 |
| VIX3M / VIX | SPY | 70 | 26.6% | 19.8% | +6.8 pts | 0.83 |
| VIX futures curve (30-day) | SPX | 157 | 13.4% | 15.6% | -2.2 pts | 0.96 |
| VIX futures curve (30-day) | SPY | 165 | 26.9% | 18.8% | +8.1 pts | 0.75 |

- **No.** Neither curve separates good trades from bad at the trade level. Three of four point the same way as the October 19 pivot (backwardation is fine to sell into), but none come close to significance.
- **The tail doesn't care about the regime.** The worst SPX trades opened in late January and February 2020 with the curve in contango or flat. No regime gate, on either curve, would have kept you out. That backs up the pivot's main point: the gate refuses bad inputs, and sizing is what handles risk.
- The Tier A study found backwardation significant (p = 0.0015) using Cboe's strategy indices over 3,945 overlapping daily windows. At the trade level the effect is the same sign but far noisier. I'm not changing any defaults on this.

## Limits

- Monthly SPX settles on the Friday open (SOQ). This settles every expiry at the close.
- No commissions. SPX is European, so no early assignment.
- Trades overlap: 1,146 trades is closer to 170 independent months.
- The parity forward ignores discounting. That's about 0.1% of the price at 30 days and doesn't change the slope buckets.
