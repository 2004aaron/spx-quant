# Tier B extended: SPY strangles, 2010 to 2023 (OptionsDX)

My instructor asked whether the backtest had enough data. The September Tier B replay covered SPY from 2019 to 2026: one bull market, two stress events, 327 trades. This run uses OptionsDX's free SPY end-of-day chains for January 2010 to December 2023. That's 1,134 trades, and it adds 2011, 2015, 2017, 2018 and 2022. Every exit is priced off a real quote instead of a model.

Setup: 16-delta short strangle, expiry nearest 45 DTE inside 30 to 60, one new 1-lot trade every 3 sessions, settled at the expiry-day close. Bid/ask fills sell the bid and buy back at the ask. The 50% target is read on the mid. Script: `research/spx-replay/tier_b_spx.py`. Raw output: [`tier-b-spy-2010-2023.json`](https://github.com/2004aaron/spx-quant/blob/research/docs/results/tier-b-spy-2010-2023.json).

## What it says

- **The profit target holds up on 14 years, with a smaller edge.** It keeps 72% of the hold-to-expiry return (September: 84%) and cuts the 5th-percentile loss by 42% (September: two thirds). It still wins the risk trade. It just costs more return than the shorter sample suggested.
- **The tail is still the whole story.** The five worst trades all opened between February 5 and 24, 2020. The worst 5% of trades lost $94,077 against $216,433 in total gains. A stop doesn't fix this. Sizing does: the 5% worst-case cap is what limits a COVID-style month.
- **Bid/ask costs are small.** One to two points of return on credit. SPY's spreads are tight enough that execution isn't the problem.
- **Bad years aren't only crashes.** 2017 lost money (-14.5% on credit, profit target) with VIX near 10. Credits were about $1.40 a share and the January 2018 melt-up ran straight through the call strikes. The floor at VIX 13 exists for this.
- **The older half was better.** 2010 to 2018: 27.5% on credit with the profit target. 2019 to 2023: 15.6%, dragged by 2020. The September number (26.8%) also included 2024 to 2026.

Backwardation entries won less often but made the most per trade, and none of them hit the COVID losses (those opened in flat or contango). That points the same way as the October 19 rules pivot. It's only 70 overlapping trades, though, and a block-shift test gives p = 0.83, so it's consistent with the pivot but proves nothing. ## Limits

- SPY, not SPX. SPY is American-style and pays dividends, so short calls can be assigned early before an ex-date. The replay ignores that.
- Trades overlap: a new one opens every 3 sessions and each runs about 25 to 43 days. 1,134 trades is closer to 170 independent months.
- No commissions. Settlement at the close.
- Only the profit-target and hold exits. The other rules stay off, as in the pivot.


Data: OptionsDX SPY end-of-day, 2010 to 2023 (free with an account). About 1.5 minutes on a laptop.
